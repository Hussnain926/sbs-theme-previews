Label literacy

How to read a supplement label in 60 seconds

Assay research team·7 min read·Reviewed for clarity, not medical advice

Most labels follow the same layout. Once you know where to look, you can compare two bottles in under a minute. Start at the top of the facts panel and work down.

1. Serving size first

Every number below depends on it. A bottle that looks cheap may need four capsules a day, not two. Divide the price by the number of servings to get a real daily cost.

If a label lists a "blend" with one total weight, you cannot know how much of each ingredient you are getting.

2. Look at the form, not just the name

Magnesium oxide and magnesium glycinate are both "magnesium", but they behave differently. Good labels name the form in brackets right after the nutrient.

Magnesium Glycinate
200 mg elemental · evening routine

3. Check for a test certificate

Third-party testing confirms that what is on the label is in the bottle. Look for a batch number you can match to a certificate.

This article is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before starting supplements, especially if you take medication.
