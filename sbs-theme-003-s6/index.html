    How long does sunscreen really last in the water?

    SPF basics · 5 min read · By the Saltlight team

    "Water resistant" is one of the most misread words on a sunscreen label. Here is what it means, and how to plan a long session at sea.

    Sunscreens are tested for water resistance in two steps: 40 minutes or 80 minutes of time in water. After that, the label only promises the SPF it had at the start of the test. That is the reason every pack says to reapply after swimming.

    Plan in 80-minute blocks

    If you surf, swim laps or spend the afternoon in a pool, think of the day in 80-minute blocks. Come in, towel off gently, reapply a generous layer and give it a few minutes before you go back out.

    Water resistant is a timer, not a promise. Set an alarm and reapply.
    Sport Stick SPF 50

    Towels take more than water

    Rubbing yourself dry removes more sunscreen than the sea does. Pat dry, then reapply. A stick is the fastest way to cover nose, cheeks and ears with wet hands.

    On land, sweat works the same way. If you have been running or playing sport for over an hour, treat it like a swim and reapply.
