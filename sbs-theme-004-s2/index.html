    The 3-minute pour over, without the fuss

    By the roasting team7 min readUpdated October 2026

    Pour over has a reputation for being fiddly. It isn't. Get the ratio right and pour slowly, and three minutes later you have the clearest cup you can make at home.

    Recipe card · one mug
    16 gCoffee
    260 gWater
    93°CTemp
    3:00Time

    What you need

    A dripper and a paper filter, a kettle (a gooseneck makes it easier), a scale and coffee roasted in the last few weeks. Grind it like coarse sand.

    The steps

    1. Rinse the filter with hot water and pour that water away.
    2. Add 16 g of coffee and shake it flat.
    3. Pour 40 g of water and wait 40 seconds while the coffee blooms.
    4. Pour the rest in slow circles, in two stages, until the scale reads 260 g.
    5. Let it drain. Aim to finish close to three minutes.
    Sour? Grind finer. Bitter? Grind coarser. That one rule fixes most cups.

    Which coffee to use

    Lighter roasts shine here. Try Copper Hour for apricot and bergamot, or Moonlit Huila if you like it sweet and round.

    The roastery

    We roast when the city goes quiet.

    Why at night

    Fewer people, more attention.

    Nightjar started in a shared roastery we could only book after 9 pm. The hours stuck. With the room to ourselves we roast slower, taste every batch and write everything down.

    Today we roast six coffees on two small drums, every Thursday, and ship them the next morning.

    01Small drums

    12 kg at most, so every batch gets watched from charge to drop.

    02Named farms

    We buy from the same producers each season and say who they are.

    03Honest freshness

    The roast date is on every bag. We never ship stock older than a week.

    From charge to doorstep

    01:00

    09:00
