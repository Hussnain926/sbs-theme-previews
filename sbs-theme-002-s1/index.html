Thrown by hand in Stoke

12 October 2026 · By Nadia Frost · 5 min read

The pottery sits behind a row of terraced houses, and you hear the wheels before you see them. Four potters work from the same clay body, a speckled stoneware that fires to a soft oatmeal.

Clay to wheel

Each vase starts as 1.4 kilos of clay, wedged by hand to push out the air. The potter centres it, opens it and pulls the walls up in three passes. A ribbed vase takes about eleven minutes.

Glaze and kiln

Pieces are bisque fired at 1000 degrees, dipped in glaze, and fired again at 1240. The glaze pools in the ribs and breaks over the rim, so no two vases match exactly.

What to do with them

Use one tall stem in a narrow neck, or a loose handful of dried grasses. Wash by hand and keep them out of the dishwasher so the glaze stays soft.

Fewer things, made to stay

Plinth started in 2020 in a rented flat with no lamp worth buying. We went looking for the small studios that still make lighting, rugs and ceramics in short runs.

Today we work with nine of them. We sell what we would put in our own rooms, and we publish where each piece is made.

The makers

Nine studios across England, Wales and Portugal. We visit each one every year.

The materials

Wool, ash, steel and stoneware. Natural and plant-dyed where we can, and no plastics in the packaging.

The guarantee

Five years on frames, cables and fittings. If it fails, we repair or replace it.
